Rehearsal dinner lawn games work best during arrivals and the open social time before or after dinner. Choose three to five familiar games, keep them close to drinks and conversation, and pause play for welcomes, toasts, and the meal. The goal is a relaxed way for families and wedding-party members to connect—not another scheduled obligation.

Because rehearsal dinners are usually smaller than the wedding, they are a good setting for games guests can revisit throughout the evening.

## Decide when games fit the evening

Games are most useful when the schedule has natural breathing room:

- During the arrival window while the wedding party returns from rehearsal
- Alongside cocktails and passed appetizers
- After a casual dinner before guests leave
- At a welcome party that includes people beyond the rehearsal group

Close or quiet the game area during toasts and announcements. If guests can see an active match behind the speaker, attention will split. A planner, host, or family member should know when to invite people back to play.

For a seated dinner with a tight timeline, games may only have a 30- to 45-minute window. In that case, three instantly recognizable choices are better than a larger collection.

## Build a game mix for two families

The guest list often combines relatives and friends who have heard about one another but have not spent much time together. Choose games that make conversation easy and do not require preassigned teams.

A balanced rehearsal dinner mix could include:

- Cornhole for a familiar head-to-head game
- Giant four-in-a-row for short rounds across generations
- A giant tumbling tower for drop-in play and spectators
- Putterball for a different kind of challenge
- Giant checkers or ring toss for a quieter option

Avoid using only games that reward throwing strength or athletic confidence. The best lineup lets an older relative, a child, and a competitive college friend all find a comfortable entry point.

## Check the venue before choosing equipment

Restaurants, private rooms, breweries, and rental homes all create different constraints. Ask the venue for the exact area where games may be placed and whether that area remains available during dinner.

Confirm:

- The surface and usable dimensions
- Loading, parking, stairs, and gate access
- Protected routes for guests and servers
- Rules for equipment on grass, patios, or indoor floors
- Outdoor lighting and closing times
- The covered or indoor weather backup
- Setup-complete and pickup windows

At a private home, protect the grill, pool, firepit, landscaping, and neighboring property from active throwing lanes. At a restaurant, make sure games do not reduce an accessible route or block service doors.

## Coordinate delivery and pickup

The rehearsal and dinner may happen at different locations. Share the actual guest-arrival time, not just the reservation time, so setup can be ready before the first person walks in.

Also consider the wedding-day schedule. Family members and the wedding party should not be responsible for returning borrowed equipment the morning of the ceremony. Full-service delivery and pickup remove that errand from an already busy weekend.

## Keep the setup wedding-ready

The rehearsal dinner can be more relaxed than the wedding without looking improvised. Use a coordinated collection and let the venue provide most of the atmosphere.

One welcome sign or simple scorecard is usually enough. Keep plastic storage tubs, shipping cases, and spare accessories out of guest areas. Place games near existing lounge furniture, but keep chairs outside throwing lanes and away from the tower's fall zone.

If the dinner has its own color palette, repeat one accent rather than personalizing every game. The setup should photograph cleanly and still feel easy to use.

## How many games do you need?

Three games suit many rehearsal dinners with up to about 75 guests. A five-game setup adds useful variety for a larger welcome party or a longer open-house format. Adjust downward when the activity area is compact or the evening includes several speeches and a formal meal.
