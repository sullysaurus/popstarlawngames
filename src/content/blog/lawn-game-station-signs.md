A useful lawn-game sign answers four questions: where to stand, what to do, how to score, and how the game ends. Most stations need fewer than 75 words. Guests should understand the first turn without reading a full rulebook.

## Lead with the action

Use a direct heading such as “Land three rings” or “Connect four.” Follow with a numbered list of no more than four steps. Put uncommon house rules in a separate line.

Avoid a long welcome paragraph before the instructions.

## Make type readable

Use high contrast, a plain font, and large body text. Do not put instructions over a busy photograph. Color can support teams, but never use color as the only way to communicate a rule.

Place signs where players wait, not where they must enter a lane to read them.

## Include only necessary scoring

For open play, guests may only need the winning condition. For a tournament, show the points, target score, time limit, and tie-break rule. Keep the posted format consistent with the host’s announcement.

## Protect the equipment and layout

Use freestanding signs or provider-approved holders. Do not tape, staple, or tie décor to rented equipment without permission. A sign base should not create a trip hazard or narrow a path.

## Add branding carefully

Event colors, a small sponsor mark, or team names can make stations feel coordinated. The rules should remain the largest and clearest information. Include a QR code only if the destination adds real value and mobile service is reliable.
