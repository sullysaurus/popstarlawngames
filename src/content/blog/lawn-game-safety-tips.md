Safe lawn-game setups use level surfaces, predictable throwing directions, clear waiting areas, and enough distance from doors, food, glass, vehicles, and seated guests. Weather and guest flow should be checked throughout the event, not only during setup.

## Inspect the surface

Look for holes, roots, slopes, mud, loose gravel, and irrigation. A board or tower that rocks during setup will not improve once guests begin playing. Move the station or close it until the surface is suitable.

## Point lanes consistently

Run multiple throwing games in parallel. Never point one lane across another, toward a walkway, or at a gathering area. Leave room behind targets for missed throws.

Keep spectators beside the lane and waiting players behind the thrower.

## Protect tall equipment

A giant tower needs a clear fall zone. Four-in-a-row frames and other standing games must remain on the approved surface and should not be climbed, leaned on, or moved by guests.

Follow the provider’s reset instructions and let adults handle heavy pieces.

## Plan for children

Use closer lines and supplied game pieces. Separate games from pools, playground exits, bikes, and high-speed activities. Supervision remains the event host’s responsibility unless staffing is included in the service agreement.

## Watch weather conditions

Rain can make grass and patios slippery. Wind can affect tall or lightweight equipment. Heat requires shade, water, and shorter rounds. Pause play when conditions change rather than waiting for a problem.

## Keep drinks and décor separate

Provide drink tables outside the play area. Keep cables, floral pieces, signs, and bags out of paths. Do not attach décor to rental equipment without approval.
