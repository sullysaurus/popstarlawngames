Lawn games need more room than the equipment footprint. Every station also needs space for the player, missed throws, waiting guests, spectators, and a safe route around play. Long-lane games may need 35 to 45 feet of total depth, while compact games still need a comfortable buffer.

Use the equipment provider’s exact measurements for the final plan.

## Group games by space type

Cornhole and other throwing games need a defined lane plus space behind both targets. Four-in-a-row and ring toss use less depth. A giant tower needs a wide fall zone on level ground. Putterball needs a controlled path for missed balls.

Sketch each station as a zone, not a rectangle around the equipment.

## Subtract fixed obstacles

Doors, stairs, trees, planters, tent poles, furniture, bars, and service paths reduce usable space. A lawn’s total dimensions do not matter if only part of it is level and connected to the event.

Ask the venue about underground irrigation, protected landscaping, and emergency access.

## Add people to the drawing

Show where two players stand, where the next group waits, and where friends watch. If those areas overlap another lane, the setup is too tight.

Keep an accessible route around the activity area.

## Plan one direction of play

Parallel lanes make movement predictable. Never aim throws toward windows, vehicles, dining, or the reception entrance. Put compact games along the social edge and long lanes on the outside.

## Measure the backup too

An indoor or covered plan may support fewer games. Decide which stations move and which close rather than squeezing the full outdoor layout inside.
