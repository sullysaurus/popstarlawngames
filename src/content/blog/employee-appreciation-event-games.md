Employee appreciation event games should feel like a benefit, not another assignment. Open-play cornhole, four-in-a-row, putterball, ring toss, yard dice, and a giant tower let people participate at their own pace while food and conversation remain central.

## Make play optional

Do not require teams, athletic clothing, or public performance. Put the games where they are visible and welcoming, then let employees choose. Spectating and socializing count as participation in the event.

## Avoid work hierarchy in the format

Random partners can mix departments without placing managers in charge of choosing teams. Skip activities that depend on sharing personal information or being judged by coworkers.

If a competition is planned, use light prizes and recognize teamwork as well as winning.

## Offer different kinds of games

Mix throwing, strategy, luck, and precision. Place a lower-impact station near seating and shade. Flexible throwing lines and rotating roles make the same setup usable by more people.

## Protect employees’ time

Schedule the event during the workday when possible and avoid asking employees to run every station. A dedicated host or vendor contact lets the planning team enjoy the gathering too.

## Keep announcements brief

Pause play for genuine recognition, then reopen the activity area. A clear timeline prevents games from competing with awards or leadership remarks.
