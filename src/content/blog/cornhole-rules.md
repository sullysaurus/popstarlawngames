Cornhole is played by tossing four bags per team toward a raised board. A bag through the hole scores three points, a bag resting on the board scores one, and a bag on the ground scores zero. Most games use cancellation scoring and continue until one team reaches 21.

Those are the essentials. For a party, consistency matters more than enforcing every tournament detail.

## Set up the court

Place the boards facing one another on flat ground, with a clear throwing lane and room behind each board. Regulation adult play uses 27 feet between the front edges of the boards, but casual players can move closer. Shortening the court is a sensible adjustment for children, mixed-age groups, or a compact event lawn.

Keep the lane out of walkways and point throws away from doors, glass, dining tables, and seated guests. Our [yard-game party guide](/blog/best-yard-games-for-parties/) has more advice for event spaces.

## Decide who throws first

A coin toss, rock-paper-scissors, or one practice bag can decide the opening team. Players stand beside their own board and alternate throws until all eight bags have been played. Partners stand at the opposite board and throw back for the next inning.

Players should stay behind the front edge of the board while releasing a bag. At a relaxed party, a quick reminder is usually enough.

## Score each inning

Use cancellation scoring: subtract the lower team’s points from the higher team’s points, then award only the difference. If one team scores seven and the other scores four, the inning is worth three points to the first team.

A bag scores three when it passes completely through the hole. It scores one when it remains on the board at the end of the inning. Bags that touch the ground before landing on the board do not count and should be removed.

## Choose a winning rule

The standard simple finish is first to 21 or more. You do not need to return to a lower score when a team goes over 21 unless everyone agrees to that house rule before play.

For a busy cocktail hour, a time limit or first-to-11 game keeps the station moving. For a tournament, write the format on a sign so every match uses the same rule.

## Make casual games welcoming

Let younger players step forward, rotate partners, and keep score optional during open play. If guests are waiting, play shorter games rather than rushing each throw.
