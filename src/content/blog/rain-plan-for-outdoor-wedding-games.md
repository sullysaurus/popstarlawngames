A workable rain plan for outdoor wedding games names the exact backup location, identifies which games fit there, and sets a deadline for the move decision. “We will bring them inside” is not enough. The venue, planner, and rental team need to agree on the surface, access, timing, and person authorized to make the call.

Build the backup while you build the outdoor plan, not after rain appears in the forecast.

## Choose a decision deadline

Ask the rental company how much notice it needs to change the setup location. The answer may depend on travel, vehicle loading, venue access, and whether the indoor layout uses a different game mix.

Then assign one decision-maker—usually the planner, venue manager, or couple's designated contact. The rental team should not receive conflicting instructions from several family members on the wedding morning.

Your plan should record:

- The time of the weather decision
- The person making it
- The forecast or venue condition that triggers the move
- The outdoor setup location
- The covered backup location
- The approved games for each plan
- Any timeline change for delivery or pickup

Rain is not the only trigger. Saturated ground, strong wind, lightning, or unsafe heat can make an outdoor setup unsuitable even when the ceremony continues outside.

## Choose games that move indoors well

Compact games are easiest to relocate. Giant four-in-a-row, ring toss, yard dice, and giant checkers can often work in a covered or indoor area with venue approval.

Cornhole may move indoors if there is room for a casual shortened lane and protection for nearby walls, windows, and guests. A giant tumbling tower needs a hard, level surface and a clear fall zone. Putterball needs a flat surface with enough room for players to stand safely.

Bocce, ladder toss, and flying-disc games are harder to reproduce indoors because they depend on distance, trajectory, or a particular surface. It may be better to reduce the backup count than to force every outdoor game into an unsuitable room.

## Measure the covered space

Get the backup area's dimensions and note fixed objects such as columns, furniture, rugs, exits, and catering stations. Confirm whether the venue allows game equipment on the floor and whether any protective material is required.

Preserve these routes first:

- Accessible paths and emergency exits
- Doors between cocktail hour and reception
- Catering and bar service paths
- Restroom access
- Space for coat storage or guest arrivals

The remaining footprint is the activity area. Draw the games into that area and leave room for spectators. Our [wedding lawn game layout guide](/blog/wedding-lawn-game-layout/) explains the same guest-flow principles for an outdoor setup.

## Coordinate with the venue

Send the rain plan to the venue before the final planning week. Ask:

1. Who can approve the indoor placement?
2. When does the backup room become available?
3. Will a ceremony or room flip use it first?
4. Are games allowed on the floor or rugs?
5. Can doors remain open between covered and indoor areas?
6. Where may the rental team unload in bad weather?
7. Is there a dry holding area during the decision window?
8. Does the venue require insurance documentation?

If the covered patio is also the rain location for the bar, buffet, or ceremony, it is not fully available for games. Ask for the revised floor plan rather than assuming everything will fit.

## Protect the guest experience

An indoor backup can still feel intentional. Use a smaller, coordinated collection and place it where guests naturally gather. Keep storage cases and wet-weather supplies out of view. One sign can explain that the games moved inside; guests do not need the story behind every adjustment.

If only two or three games fit, choose variety over quantity. A quick strategy game, a social anchor, and one precision game will serve more people than three similar throwing stations.

Do not reopen an outdoor station simply because the rain stops. The surface may remain slippery or soft, and moving equipment twice can disrupt the reception.

## Ask the rental company before booking

Confirm what happens when the location changes:

- Is the indoor setup included?
- Which games cannot be used in the backup area?
- Who protects the venue floor?
- Can the number of games change?
- Are delays, second moves, or late pickup charged separately?
- What happens if the event is postponed or canceled?
