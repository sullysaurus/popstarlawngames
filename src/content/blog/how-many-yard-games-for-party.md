Most casual parties work well with three to five yard games. Three creates a clear activity zone for a smaller gathering, while five adds variety and capacity for a larger crowd. Very large events may need duplicate stations rather than an ever-longer list of different games.

## Start with active guests

Do not assume everyone will play at once. Some guests will eat, talk, watch, or arrive later. Estimate how many people will be near the activity area during the busiest window.

A useful planning range is one station for roughly 25 to 35 people in that area, then adjust for game speed and the event format.

## Count stations, not just products

One cornhole set creates one station. Extra boards or duplicate compact games create more simultaneous play. At a large event, capacity often matters more than variety.

Fast-turn games serve more guests than long matches. Short formats can increase throughput without adding equipment.

## Let space set the ceiling

Every station needs player, waiting, and spectator room. Long lanes cannot overlap with doors or food lines. Three properly spaced games feel more active than six squeezed into unsafe corners.

## Adjust for the format

Open play spreads demand across the event. A tournament needs enough courts to complete its bracket on time. A station rotation needs one activity per team or a planned break station.

## Build a varied mix

Combine a social anchor such as cornhole, a strategy game, a dexterity game, and a compact option. Avoid buying capacity by repeating the same skill at every station.
