The standard adult cornhole setup places the front edges of the two boards 27 feet apart. Players pitch from beside their own board, so a complete court also needs room behind and beside both boards. Junior and casual games can use a shorter distance.

## Measure the board fronts

Measure from the front edge of one board to the front edge of the other, not from the holes or back legs. Align the boards so their centers face one another and the lane does not angle through guest traffic.

Mark foul lines at each board’s front edge if players need a visual reminder.

## Allow more than 27 feet

The official gap is only the playing lane. Add space behind each board for players and missed bags, plus room along the sides for waiting teams and spectators. A party court may need roughly 40 feet of total depth to feel comfortable, depending on the setting.

Do not put that extra space in a doorway, bar line, or catering path.

## Shorten the distance when appropriate

Children, beginners, and guests with limited mobility can throw from a closer mark. A short court also fits some backyard and cocktail-hour settings better. The important part is agreeing on the line before a scored game.

For a tournament, use consistent distances within each division or format.

## Choose the safest direction

Point both boards away from windows, cars, water, seated guests, and entrances. Multiple courts should run parallel so throws travel in predictable directions. Leave a buffer between courts.

## Check the surface

Boards need stable footing. Avoid steep slopes, soft mud, and areas where bags can roll into traffic. After rain, move or close the court if footing is unsafe.
