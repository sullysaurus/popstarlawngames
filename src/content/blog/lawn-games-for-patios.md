Patios and courtyards can be excellent game spaces because the surface is level and close to the party. Giant four-in-a-row, ring toss, putterball, yard dice, and some tower setups fit well when the venue approves the equipment and doors remain clear.

## Confirm the surface rules

Ask whether wood, rolling balls, and game frames may sit directly on the floor. Stone, tile, decking, artificial turf, and historic surfaces have different protection needs. Use only pads or mats approved by the provider and venue.

## Protect every doorway

Measure doors in both open and closed positions. Keep the routes to restrooms, exits, the bar, and catering completely outside play. Courtyards often look larger when empty than they feel once furniture and guests arrive.

## Choose controlled pieces

Four-in-a-row keeps discs inside the frame. Ring toss uses a short, visible lane. Putterball can work when missed balls cannot roll into stairs or dining. Yard dice should be rolled gently in a bounded area.

Full-distance cornhole may not fit; a shortened social court can work if everyone uses the same mark.

## Manage sound and fall zones

Hard surfaces amplify dropped blocks and dice. Keep a tower away from dining, glass, and quiet program areas. Confirm venue noise expectations, especially in enclosed courtyards.

## Build a compact lineup

Two or three stations with space to breathe are better than a crowded patio. Use the adjoining lawn for long lanes when available, keeping both zones visually connected.
