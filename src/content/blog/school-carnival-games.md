School carnival games need quick turns, visible rules, and predictable resets. Ring toss, cornhole, four-in-a-row speed rounds, yard-dice challenges, and putting stations can work when each child receives the same fixed number of attempts.

## Design for line speed

Use three throws, rings, or putts per turn. Set a target time and test the station before opening. A full game of cornhole or four-in-a-row may be too slow during peak attendance; use a single-round challenge instead.

Create a separate entry and exit so finished players do not walk back through the queue.

## Adjust by age

Mark two or three throwing lines and label them by grade range or player choice. Keep scoring simple enough that volunteers apply it consistently. Younger children can earn participation credit rather than needing a difficult target.

## Write volunteer instructions

Each station needs a one-page guide covering setup, attempts, scoring, reset, safety, and who to contact. Rotate volunteers and plan breaks.

Do not rely on one person remembering verbal rules for an entire shift.

## Protect the layout

Point throws away from crowds, classroom doors, food, parking, and other games. Use barriers or floor marks to separate the queue from play. Adult volunteers handle heavy or tall equipment resets.

## Decide the prize system

Tickets or stamps are easier to manage than different prizes at every station. If performance changes the reward, post the exact threshold and include an age-appropriate success level.
