The best indoor lawn games have compact footprints, controlled pieces, and no need for a long throw. Giant four-in-a-row, ring toss, putterball, yard dice, and some giant-tower setups can move indoors when the venue approves the equipment and the floor is level.

Do not assume an outdoor plan will fit inside without measuring.

## Start with venue permission

Ask which rooms, floors, and equipment types are approved. Historic venues, galleries, and ballrooms may restrict rolling pieces, wood contact, or tall equipment. Confirm whether protective mats are required and who may move the games.

Record access routes, door widths, elevators, and setup windows before event day.

## Choose compact games

Four-in-a-row and ring toss need less depth than a full cornhole court. Putterball can work in a defined lane if missed balls remain contained. Yard dice need a clear roll area and a rule against hard throws.

A giant tower needs a stable, level surface plus an open fall zone. Keep it away from glass, bars, floral displays, and dining tables.

## Protect guest flow

Indoor rooms concentrate movement. Keep games outside the routes to restrooms, exits, the bar, catering service, and coat check. Use furniture or floor markers to show where play begins and where spectators stand.

Never narrow an emergency exit path to create a game station.

## Adapt the rules

Shorten throws, use lower-energy motions, and reduce the number of active stations if the room is tight. A compact setup that feels intentional is safer and more inviting than squeezing every outdoor game into a backup room.
