To run a smooth cornhole tournament, choose the bracket before guests arrive, set one ruleset, estimate how many matches each court can handle, and assign one person to record results. For social events, single elimination or pool play followed by a short final usually works better than a long double-elimination bracket.

## Pick the format from the available time

Single elimination is the simplest: a team leaves the bracket after one loss. It creates excitement and a clear finish, but some guests may play only once.

Pool play guarantees more games. Divide teams into small groups, let each team play the others in its group, then advance the group winners to a semifinal or final. Use this when participation matters more than speed.

Double elimination gives every team a second chance but adds many matches. Choose it only when cornhole is the event’s main activity and the schedule has room.

## Estimate court capacity

A casual first-to-21 match often needs 15 to 25 minutes, including the changeover. Shorter first-to-11 games can move faster. Weather, player experience, and socializing all affect pace, so include a buffer.

Divide the total match count by the number of courts to estimate rounds. Do not promise a one-hour tournament that requires each court to host five full matches.

## Publish one ruleset

Decide the board spacing, winning score, cancellation scoring, foul rule, and whether a team must win by two. Put the rules where players check in. Our [cornhole rules guide](/blog/cornhole-rules/) gives a practical baseline.

For mixed-age groups, let children or less mobile players use a closer line. Apply the adjustment consistently.

## Build and label the bracket

Number the teams and courts. A printed bracket works for a small tournament; a shared digital bracket can help at a larger event. Leave space to record scores and the next court assignment.

Call the next matchup before the current match ends. A five-minute no-show rule prevents the entire schedule from waiting on one team.

## Give the tournament a host

One person should answer rules questions, update results, and announce the final. The host should not also be responsible for catering, registration, and the event timeline.
