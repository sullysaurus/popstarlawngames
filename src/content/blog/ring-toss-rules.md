In a simple ring toss game, each player throws the same number of rings from a marked line. A ring that lands completely around a peg earns that peg’s point value. Add the points after every player has thrown; the highest total wins.

Because ring-toss sets vary, use the point labels and throwing distance supplied with the equipment as the final authority.

## Set a fair throwing line

Place the target on a level surface and mark a line that suits the group. Adults can throw from the standard distance for the set. Children, older guests, or casual players can use a closer line.

Keep the target away from feet and walkways. Players waiting for a turn should stand behind the thrower, not beside the pegs.

## Choose the number of rings

Give every player three to five throws per round. Three keeps a busy party station moving; five gives a more competitive group time to adjust.

Use the same ring set and line for everyone in a scored round. During open play, flexible distances are fine as long as guests are not comparing formal scores.

## Score clean rings

A ring must settle around a peg to score. A ring leaning against a peg or resting on the base does not count unless the posted house rules say otherwise. If two rings interfere, leave them in place until the round ends.

For young children, simplify scoring by awarding one point for any successful ring.

## Try event-friendly formats

For team play, alternate throwers and combine scores. For a quick tie-break, each player throws one ring and the highest peg wins. For a station rotation, give teams 60 seconds and count total successful rings.

At a wedding or company party, avoid elimination. Short repeatable rounds let guests join without waiting for a long bracket.

## Reset and inspect

Collect rings only after the final throw. Check that the base remains flat and the pegs are secure before the next round. Move the game indoors or under cover if rain makes the throwing surface slippery.
